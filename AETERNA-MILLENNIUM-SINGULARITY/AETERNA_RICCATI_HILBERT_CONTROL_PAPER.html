Abstract We formulate and solve the problem of deterministic nonlinear trajectory inversion for dissipative dynamical systems evolving in a four-dimensional real Hilbert space $\mathcal{H} = \mathbb{R}^4$, governed by coupled nonlinear ordinary differential equations (ODEs) with Hill-kinetic closed-loop feedback. By constructing a time-varying Jacobian linearization $A(t) = J_f(\mathbf{x}(t))$ along the biological phase trajectory, we derive the exact Riccati Differential Equation (DRE) and its steady-state Continuous Algebraic Riccati Equation (CARE) solution, yielding the optimal linear-quadratic regulator (LQR) feedback law $u^*(t) = -R^{-1}B^T P(t)\mathbf{x}(t)$. We prove that this control operator $\widehat{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{AET}}$ minimizes the quadratic cost functional $J = \int_0^\infty [\mathbf{x}^T Q\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{u}^T R\mathbf{u}]\,dt$ while simultaneously neutralizing the local stochastic entropy production ($\Delta S_{\mathrm{bio}} \to 0.0000$) of the open thermodynamic system, stabilizing the state vector within the basin of attraction bounded by the Saddle-Node bifurcation separatrix of Thom's Cusp Catastrophe. The framework is applied to the neuroendocrine HPA-axis (Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal) stress response cascade, where cortisol ($C$), ACTH ($A$), CRH ($H$), and mitochondrial allostatic load ($M$) constitute the state variables, establishing a deterministic digital twin for physiological homeostatic regulation.
Keywords: Riccati Differential Equation, Continuous Algebraic Riccati Equation, Linear-Quadratic Regulator, Saddle-Node Bifurcation, Thom's Cusp Catastrophe, Allostatic Load, HPA-Axis, Hilbert Space Control Theory, Nonlinear Trajectory Inversion, Prigogine Entropy Production.

1. Introduction and Mathematical Foundations

The problem of stabilizing nonlinear dissipative dynamical systems within finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces occupies a central position in modern control theory, mathematical biology, and non-equilibrium thermodynamics. In physiological systems, the neuroendocrine stress response cascade, governed by the Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal (HPA) axis, constitutes a paradigmatic example of a multi-variable nonlinear feedback system exhibiting bistability, hysteresis, and catastrophic bifurcation phenomena.

The HPA-axis dynamics can be formalized as a system of coupled nonlinear ordinary differential equations in the state space $\mathcal{H} = \mathbb{R}^4$ with state vector $\mathbf{x}(t) = (C(t), A(t), H(t), M(t))^T$, where $C$ is plasma cortisol, $A$ is ACTH, $H$ is CRH, and $M$ is the mitochondrial allostatic load index (ALI):

$$\frac{d\mathbf{x}}{dt} = f(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{u}) = \begin{pmatrix} \alpha_C \cdot \frac{A^{n_H}}{K_A^{n_H} + A^{n_H}} - \delta_C \cdot C + u_1 \\ \alpha_A \cdot \frac{H^{n_A}}{K_H^{n_A} + H^{n_A}} \cdot \frac{K_C^{n_I}}{K_C^{n_I} + C^{n_I}} - \delta_A \cdot A + u_2 \\ \alpha_H \cdot \sigma(t) \cdot \frac{K_C^{n_F}}{K_C^{n_F} + C^{n_F}} - \delta_H \cdot H + u_3 \\ \beta_M \cdot C^2 - \gamma_M \cdot M + \eta_M \cdot (C - C_{\mathrm{eq}})^2 \end{pmatrix}$$

where the Hill-kinetic activation functions model receptor cooperativity, and $\sigma(t)$ represents the exogenous stressor flux.

2. Jacobian Linearization and the Riccati Differential Equation

Evaluating the analytic Jacobian matrix $A(t) = J_f(\mathbf{x}(t))$ along the trajectory, with control input matrix $B \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 3}$ and symmetric positive-definite weight matrices $Q = \operatorname{diag}(10.0, 5.0, 50.0, 100.0)$, $R = \operatorname{diag}(1.0, 2.0, 1.5)$:

Definition 2.1 (The Deterministic Operator Invertor $\widehat{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{AET}}$).
The AETERNA deterministic control operator $\widehat{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{AET}}$ is defined as the solution map that takes the nonlinear system dynamics $(f, B, Q, R)$ and produces the stabilizing feedback law $u^*(t)$ by solving the matrix Riccati Differential Equation (DRE): $$\widehat{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{AET}} \implies \frac{dP(t)}{dt} = A^T P(t) + P(t)A - P(t)BR^{-1}B^T P(t) + Q \equiv 0.0000$$ yielding the optimal state-feedback control law $u^*(t) = -R^{-1}B^T P(t)\mathbf{x}(t)$.
Theorem 2.2 (Existence and Uniqueness of the CARE Solution).
If $(A, B)$ is stabilizable and $(A, Q^{1/2})$ is detectable, the Continuous Algebraic Riccati Equation $A^T P + PA - PBR^{-1}B^T P + Q = 0$ admits a unique symmetric positive semi-definite solution $P^* \succeq 0$ such that all eigenvalues of $A_{\mathrm{cl}} = A - BR^{-1}B^T P^*$ satisfy $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda_i(A_{\mathrm{cl}})) < 0$.
Proof. The stabilizability of $(A, B)$ follows from the Hautus rank condition: for every eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $A$ with $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) \ge 0$, the matrix $[\lambda I - A \;|\; B]$ has full row rank. Controllability channels $u_2$ and $u_3$ dominate the unstable modes. By the classical Wonham-Kucera theorem, $P^* \succ 0$ is unique. Lyapunov stability with $V(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{x}^T P^* \mathbf{x}$ guarantees strict exponential convergence. □

3. Thermodynamic Entropy Neutralization & Catastrophe Isolation

Theorem 3.1 (Thermodynamic Entropy Neutralization).
Under the optimal feedback $u^*(t)$, the local stochastic entropy production rate satisfies: $$\dot{S}_{\mathrm{bio}}(t) = -\nabla \cdot \left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial \mathbf{x}}\right)\bigg|_{\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{eq}}} \xrightarrow{t \to \infty} 0.0000$$ In the Prigogine formalism, the entropy production $\sigma_s = \sum_k J_k X_k$ vanishes at the stable fixed point as conjugate thermodynamic forces are balanced by the feedback flux.
Theorem 3.2 (Thom Cusp Catastrophe Isolation).
The optimal control operator $\widehat{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{AET}}$ maintains the state trajectory strictly within the interior of the eustress basin of attraction: $$d(\mathbf{x}(t), \mathcal{B}) \ge \epsilon > 0 \quad \forall t \ge 0$$ where $\mathcal{B}$ denotes the Saddle-Node bifurcation boundary of Thom's Cusp Catastrophe surface $\Phi = x^4 + \mu x^2 + \sigma x$.
