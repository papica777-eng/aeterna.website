Abstract Sustained propulsion at $v = 0.20c$ over a 197-year interstellar transit requires a self-replenishing energy source that harvests fuel from the interstellar medium itself. We present the full relativistic kinematic formulation of the AETERNA Exodus Bussard Magnetic Scoop, operating at cruise velocity $v = 5.996 \times 10^7$ m/s with Lorentz factor $\gamma = 1.02062$. A superconducting magnetic funnel of radius $R = 100{,}000$ km ($\pi R^2 = 3.1416 \times 10^{16}$ m$^2$) sweeps interstellar hydrogen at density $n_H = 10^6$ protons/m$^3$ ($\rho_{\text{ISM}} = 1.673 \times 10^{-21}$ kg/m$^3$), collecting $\dot{m} = 3{,}150.6$ kg/s of proton mass. Full rest-mass annihilation ($E = mc^2$) yields $P_{\text{raw}} = 2.831 \times 10^{20}$ W ($283.1$ Exawatts). After deducting magnetic field maintenance ($1.50 \times 10^{18}$ W) and relativistic ramjet drag ($P_{\text{drag}} = \dot{m}v^2 = 1.132 \times 10^{19}$ W), the net power surplus is $P_{\text{net}} = 2.703 \times 10^{20}$ W, confirming energetic self-sustainability. Waste heat dissipation ($10\%$ thermal loss $= 2.8 \times 10^{19}$ W) is achieved via liquid tin micro-droplet radiators at $T = 2000$ K ($\epsilon = 0.95$), radiating $q = \epsilon\sigma T^4 = 8.619 \times 10^5$ W/m$^2$, deployed as a dual-face planar sheet of length 5,000 km. 1. Relativistic Kinematics at 0.20c

The Exodus vessel cruises at velocity $v = 0.20c = 0.20 \times 299{,}792{,}458 = 59{,}958{,}491.6$ m/s. The Lorentz factor is:

$$\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - \beta^2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - 0.04}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{0.96}} \approx 1.02062$$

At this velocity, relativistic corrections are modest ($\gamma - 1 \approx 2.06\%$) but must be tracked precisely for energy accounting over 197 years.

2. Magnetic Funnel Geometry and Swept Volume

The superconducting magnetic scoop deploys a toroidal field geometry with effective capture radius $R = 100{,}000$ km $= 10^8$ m. The frontal cross-sectional area is:

$$A = \pi R^2 = \pi \times (10^8)^2 = 3.1416 \times 10^{16} \text{ m}^2$$

The volumetric sweep rate at cruise velocity is:

$$\dot{V} = A \cdot v = 3.1416 \times 10^{16} \times 5.996 \times 10^7 \approx 1.884 \times 10^{24} \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$$

3. Interstellar Medium Hydrogen Collection

The warm neutral interstellar medium has a proton number density of $n_H = 10^6$ protons/m$^3$ (equivalent to $\sim 1$ atom/cm$^3$). The mass density is:

$$\rho_{\text{ISM}} = n_H \cdot m_p = 10^6 \times 1.67262 \times 10^{-27} = 1.673 \times 10^{-21} \text{ kg/m}^3$$

The hydrogen mass collection rate is:

$$\dot{m} = \dot{V} \cdot \rho_{\text{ISM}} = 1.884 \times 10^{24} \times 1.673 \times 10^{-21} \approx 3{,}150.6 \text{ kg/s}$$

4. Rest-Mass Annihilation Power Budget

Assuming complete proton-antiproton annihilation (or equivalent catalyzed fusion/annihilation process), the raw power output is:

$$P_{\text{raw}} = \dot{m} c^2 = 3{,}150.6 \times (2.998 \times 10^8)^2 = 3{,}150.6 \times 8.988 \times 10^{16} \approx 2.831 \times 10^{20} \text{ W}$$
Theorem 4.1 (Energetic Self-Sustainability). The net power yield after deducting all parasitic loads is: $$P_{\text{net}} = P_{\text{raw}} - P_{\text{field}} - P_{\text{drag}} = 2.831 \times 10^{20} - 1.50 \times 10^{18} - 1.132 \times 10^{19} \approx 2.703 \times 10^{20} \text{ W}$$ Since $P_{\text{net}} > 0$, the system is strictly self-sustaining: no onboard fuel reserve depletion occurs during the 197-year transit.

5. Stefan-Boltzmann Liquid Tin Droplet Radiator

Waste heat from the annihilation engine (assumed 10% thermal loss) produces a thermal load of:

$$Q_{\text{waste}} = 0.10 \times P_{\text{raw}} = 2.831 \times 10^{19} \text{ W} \approx 2.8 \times 10^{19} \text{ W}$$

Liquid tin micro-droplets ($\epsilon = 0.95$) are ejected into a planar sheet radiating at $T = 2000$ K. The Stefan-Boltzmann radiated flux is:

$$q_{\text{rad}} = \epsilon \sigma T^4 = 0.95 \times 5.670 \times 10^{-8} \times (2000)^4 = 0.95 \times 5.670 \times 10^{-8} \times 1.6 \times 10^{13} \approx 8.619 \times 10^5 \text{ W/m}^2$$

The total required radiating surface area is:

$$A_{\text{rad}} = \frac{Q_{\text{waste}}}{q_{\text{rad}}} = \frac{2.8 \times 10^{19}}{8.619 \times 10^5} \approx 3.249 \times 10^{13} \text{ m}^2$$

For a dual-face sheet of length $L = 5{,}000$ km $= 5 \times 10^6$ m:

$$W = \frac{A_{\text{rad}}}{2L} = \frac{3.249 \times 10^{13}}{10^7} \approx 3.249 \times 10^6 \text{ m} = 3{,}249 \text{ km}$$
